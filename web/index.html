Civis Index · v1 · A response to the BCW

Where do people actually live the best lives?

29 advanced economies, 9 domains, 24 indicators, 1990–present. A revision of the BCW Index that gives equal weight to material conditions, health, knowledge, safety, equality, freedom, work-life, environment, and subjective wellbeing, instead of letting outcome-quantity drown out outcome-quality.

The original BCW takes 12 indicators, z-scores each, and reports the median per country. Six of those 12 measure something close to "rich, developed country" (GDP, household consumption, broadband, internet, infant mortality, maternal mortality), so the index rewards wealth six times. It has no measure of inequality, no measure of political freedom, no measure of work-life balance, and no measure of environmental sustainability. The result: Hong Kong ranked second.

The Civis Index takes 24 indicators across 9 equally-weighted domains, and aggregates them hierarchically: indicator z-scores → mean within domain → mean across domains. Correlated indicators within a domain don't double-count, and missing a domain hurts you proportionally to one ninth of the score, not one twelfth of the variable list.

    Z-scores are winsorized at ±2.5σ to prevent extreme outliers from dominating. Equal weights are a starting point, not a claim of objectivity. Adjust the domain weights below to see how the ranking shifts under your values.
